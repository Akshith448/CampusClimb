We evaluated our framework using a rigorous held-out test methodology across three undergraduate Computer Science courses: Operating Systems (OS, n=95 in test, 135 total), Computer Networks (CN, n=62 in test, 88 total), and Database Management Systems (DBMS, n=72 in test, 103 total), resulting in a final gold test set of 229 questions (with an additional 97 samples held-out in dev for configuration selection, totaling 326 samples in the gold mapping pool). Sweep tuning on the development set led to the selection of a hybrid ensemble combining raw Sentence-Transformer embeddings and TF-IDF similarity with weight α=0.6 and no abstaining. On the held-out test set, the Hybrid (α=0.6) framework achieved a Top-1 accuracy of 0.5677 (95% Wilson CI: [0.5029, 0.6302]) and a Top-3 accuracy of 0.7205. McNemar's test on Top-1 predictions shows that the Hybrid (α=0.6) statistically outperforms the original CAPT-M Sentence-Transformer baseline with adaptive abstaining (Top-1: 0.5197, chi2=4.8400, p=0.0278), TF-IDF alone (Top-1: 0.5022, chi2=7.2581, p=7.0583e-03), and unsupervised LDA alone (Top-1: 0.2227, chi2=57.2569, p=3.82e-14). For semantic deduplication, we tuned the cosine similarity threshold on a 30-pair dev set (yielding a threshold of 0.75) and evaluated it on a 60-pair clean held-out eval set. At threshold 0.75, the framework achieved a full eval-set F1 score of 0.7013 (95% Wilson CI: [0.5763, 0.8021]; Precision: 0.5400, Recall: 1.0000) and a hard-case eval-set F1 score of 0.5965 (95% Wilson CI: [0.4426, 0.7335]; Precision: 0.4250, Recall: 1.0000). This tuned threshold represents a substantial improvement in duplicate recall compared to the original fixed 0.85 threshold, which suffered from high false negatives. However, a significant limitation was identified on the hard-case eval-set: the system achieved a false-negative rate of 0% (perfect recall) but a true-negative rate (TNR) of 0% (23 out of 23 true hard negatives misclassified as false duplicates at threshold 0.75), indicating the system currently cannot correctly reject any hard negative pair in this near-threshold region. This evaluation is explicitly scoped to three JNTUH-format undergraduate CS courses; generalization to other disciplines or less structured course materials requires larger, more diverse benchmarks.
